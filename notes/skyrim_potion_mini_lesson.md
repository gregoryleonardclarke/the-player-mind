## Three teaching points
1. Loss aversion is a tendency in some risky decisions to experience a loss as more consequential than a comparable gain. Owning a rare potion and consuming it can make its immediate loss salient.
2. Anticipated regret can shape a decision before we make it. In a game, the hypothetical harder fight tomorrow can make spending a rare item today feel risky.
3. Uncertainty about future demands creates a trade-off: saving an item preserves future flexibility but forgoes its useful benefit now. These are interpretations of a game behaviour, *not* direct experimental proof that loss aversion causes Skyrim potion hoarding.

## Literature used for claims
- Daniel Kahneman & Amos Tversky (1979), “Prospect Theory: An Analysis of Decision under Risk,” *Econometrica*, 47(2), 263–292. https://doi.org/10.2307/1914185
- Marcel Zeelenberg (1999), “Anticipated regret, expected feedback and behavioral decision making,” *Journal of Behavioral Decision Making*, 12(2), 93–106. https://doi.org/10.1002/(SICI)1099-0771(199906)12:2%3C93::AID-BDM311%3E3.0.CO;2-S

Ever finished Skyrim with a bag full of potions you'd been saving for a “better time”?

Loss aversion and anticipated regret are two researched ideas from decision psychology that may help explain *why* we sometimes hang on to rare resources. But saving for later also has a cost: you miss the help you could have had right now.
